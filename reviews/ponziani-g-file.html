You shattered his kingside — and unsealed a file at your own king

The structure is genuinely worse for Black — but structure is a slow asset and the open g‑file is a fast one. Recapturing with the g‑pawn hands Black a highway that points directly at g1. Within five moves his rooks are on g8 and g7; your doubled‑pawn target never becomes reachable.

Keeping queens on with 14.Qg3 preserves the pressure without opening anything — 14.Qg3 Bd7 15.Kh1 h6 16.Bxh6 keeps every piece pointed the right way. You trade when the resulting position is one you can play, not merely one where the pawn count looks prettier.

What to take away

Two things, and deliberately not a list of every error.

Habit · every move

Ask what your opponent's last move was for

You never once responded to Black's plan. …Rg8 and …Rg7 are not quiet moves — they are an announcement. Before you commit to your own idea, name in one sentence what the opponent is threatening. If you can't, that's the move to spend time on.

Habit · before trading

A trade that wrecks their structure can still open your king

14.Bxf6 gave Black doubled f‑pawns and gave you a lost game. Structural damage is a slow asset; an open file next to a king is a fast one. When the two point in opposite directions, speed wins.

One concrete drill

You've played the Ponziani in three of your last ten games and scored 1/3. In all three, the same thing happens: you build the big centre, your opponent steps into it, and you develop instead of taking. Next five Ponziani games — when a black piece lands on d4 or e5, calculate the capture first, every time, before you look at anything else.
